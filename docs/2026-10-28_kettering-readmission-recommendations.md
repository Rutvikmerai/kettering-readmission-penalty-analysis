# Recommendation: Where Kettering Health Should Focus Readmission Reduction

**Basis:** Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP), Fiscal Year 2027 results (discharges July 2023 to June 2025)

*This is an independent portfolio analysis built only on public CMS data. It was not requested or reviewed by Kettering Health. All dollar figures are estimates.*

---

## The Answer

**Focus on two targets: heart failure at four hospitals, and the cardiac program at Kettering Health Dayton. Together they account for over 80 percent of Kettering's estimated readmission penalty.**

| Priority | Target | Estimated annual penalty | Share of total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heart failure at Main Campus, Miamisburg, Soin Medical Center, and Troy | $180,000 to $257,000 | about 50% |
| 2 | Heart attack and bypass surgery at Kettering Health Dayton | $116,000 to $165,000 | about 32% |
| | **Both targets** | **$295,000 to $422,000** | **over 80%** |
| | All Kettering hospitals, all conditions | $362,000 to $518,000 | 100% |

---

## Why These Two

### Priority 1: Heart failure
- **It is Kettering's distinctive problem.** 6 of 7 Kettering hospitals are penalized for heart failure, compared with 57 percent of other Ohio hospitals. On most other conditions, Kettering is at or better than the state.
- **It is half of the penalty**, because Medicare spends more on heart failure stays than on most other measured conditions, so each excess readmission costs more.
- **Four hospitals carry nearly all of it.** Main Campus alone is an estimated $102,000 to $146,000. Miamisburg, Soin Medical Center, and Troy add $19,000 to $43,000 each. Hamilton and Greene Memorial are penalized by very small margins (under $1,500 each).
- **It is not new.** In the earlier Fiscal Year 2026 data (July 2021 to June 2024), Soin Medical Center had the worst heart failure readmission ratio in Ohio and Main Campus ranked 6th of 107. The two periods use different methods and cannot be compared point for point, but the problem has persisted across both.

**Start at Main Campus.** It is the largest single driver in the system and the largest hospital, so improvements there move the most dollars and the most patients.

### Priority 2: The cardiac program at Kettering Health Dayton
- **Heart attack at Dayton is the single largest gap from peers in the system:** a readmission ratio of 1.170 compared with a peer median of 0.996, or about 17 percent more readmissions than similar hospitals. Estimated penalty: $83,000 to $119,000.
- **Bypass surgery at Dayton is also penalized** (1.061 compared with 1.006), adding $32,000 to $46,000.
- **This is a one-site, one-service-line problem**, which makes it easier to address than a system-wide pattern: one cardiology team and one set of discharge processes.
- **It also appeared in the earlier data.** Dayton ranked 2nd worst of 72 Ohio hospitals for heart attack readmissions in Fiscal Year 2026, and 5th of 40 for bypass surgery.

---

## What Not to Prioritize, and Why

**Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease looks worse than it is.** In the Fiscal Year 2026 data, four Kettering hospitals had the four worst ratios in Ohio. But Medicare weights each condition by how much it spends on it, and this condition is a small share of spending. It contributes only about 8 percent of the estimated penalty ($28,000 to $40,000). Choosing targets from rankings alone would have put effort in the wrong place.

**Pneumonia is a watch item, not a target.** It was a strength in the Fiscal Year 2026 data, but Hamilton and Dayton are now penalized for it ($25,000 to $36,000 combined). Track it, but it does not yet justify a dedicated effort.

---

## Options for the Quality Team to Evaluate

These are commonly used approaches to reducing heart failure and cardiac readmissions. Which ones fit depends on what Kettering already has in place, which public data cannot show.

1. **Follow-up appointment before discharge.** Schedule a clinic visit within 7 days of discharge for every heart failure and heart attack patient.
2. **Medication review at discharge.** Pharmacist review of heart medications and a plain-language explanation for the patient and family.
3. **Follow-up phone call within 48 to 72 hours** to catch symptoms, missed prescriptions, or confusion early.
4. **Home monitoring for higher-risk heart failure patients**, such as daily weight checks, since sudden weight gain is an early warning sign.
5. **Learn from pneumonia.** Kettering performs well on pneumonia readmissions at most hospitals. Whatever discharge practices drive that result may transfer to heart failure.

---

## What the Penalty Number Does and Does Not Mean

- **The dollars are modest compared with Kettering's total revenue.** The stronger reasons to act are patient outcomes, the cost of readmissions under Medicare Advantage contracts (Medicare Advantage patients now count in the readmission ratios), and the risk that the penalty grows toward its 3 percent cap.
- **Improvement shows up with a delay.** Penalties are based on a rolling two-year window of past discharges. Changes made now will affect penalties in later fiscal years, not the current one.
- **The penalty is relative.** It depends on how Kettering compares with its peer group, so peers improving at the same time can reduce the benefit.

---

## Limitations

1. **Dollar figures are estimates.** The penalty rates are official CMS figures. Converting them to dollars required assuming that base Medicare payments are 70 to 100 percent of total 2024 Medicare payments. Kettering's finance team can replace this range with the exact figure from its own records or its Hospital-Specific Report.
2. **Some hospital results combine several campuses.** Satellite campuses (for example, Washington Township, Huber, and Preble) report under their parent hospital's CMS number.
3. **Public data cannot explain causes.** It shows where readmissions are high, not why. Patient-level data (discharge destination, time to follow-up, reason for readmission) would be needed to choose specific interventions.
4. **Fiscal Year 2026 and Fiscal Year 2027 results are not directly comparable,** because CMS shortened the measurement period from three years to two and added Medicare Advantage patients.

---

## Suggested Next Steps

1. Confirm the estimated dollar exposure with the finance team using actual base payment figures.
2. Pull patient-level readmission data for Main Campus heart failure and Dayton heart attack patients to find common patterns (time of discharge, discharge destination, days to readmission).
3. Compare discharge processes for pneumonia and heart failure at the same hospitals.
4. Track quarterly readmission rates for the two targets internally, without waiting for annual CMS results.
